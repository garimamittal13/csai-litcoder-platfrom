Five MI Mechanisms

Each mechanism was evaluated with multiple variants using ridge regression on parcellated fMRI data.

01

Sparse Autoencoders

Qwen 2.5-0.5B · 24 layers · d=896

Sparse autoencoders decompose dense activations into interpretable, sparse features. We compare five SAE architectures applied to Qwen 2.5-0.5B hidden states and test whether sparsified representations improve fMRI alignment.

Variants

Vanilla ★ Gated ★ JumpReLU BatchTopK Hierarchical

Key Findings

  • Vanilla & Gated SAE at Layer 11 peak at R = 0.079
  • Zero-shot EN→CN transfer: R = 0.064
  • SAEs outperform normalization variants by ~75%
  • Sparsity improves cross-lingual alignment compared to dense activations

02

Normalization Layers

Qwen 2.5-0.5B · 24 layers · d=896

Different normalization strategies alter the distribution of hidden states. We swap the default normalization in Qwen 2.5-0.5B and evaluate how each affects alignment with brain responses.

Variants

LayerNorm RMSNorm ScaleNorm DeepNorm BatchNorm

Key Findings

  • All variants plateau at R ≈ 0.045 — no significant differentiation
  • SAE representations outperform all norm variants by ~75%
  • Normalization choice alone does not drive brain alignment
  • Suggests feature sparsity matters more than activation distribution

03

Attention Mechanisms

MiniTransformer · d=128 · 4 heads · 4 layers

We compare four multi-head attention variants and measure their cross-lingual brain alignment. A controlled MiniTransformer (d=128) isolates the effect of attention design from model scale.

Variants

MHA ★ MQA GQA ★ MHLA

Key Findings

  • MHA/GQA most stable cross-lingually
  • English peak: R ≈ 0.71
  • Chinese (ZH): R ≈ 0.16 — significant language gap
  • Full-attention variants preserve more cross-lingual structure

04

Activation Functions

TinyCausalTransformer · tiny d=64/2L · small d=96/3L

Activation functions govern nonlinear transformations inside FFN blocks. We swap activations in a controlled TinyCausalTransformer and measure the effect on brain alignment and cross-language transfer.

Variants

ReLU GELU GEGLU SwiGLU SoLU ★

Key Findings

  • SoLU is the only variant with positive cross-language transfer (+0.006)
  • GEGLU and SwiGLU show stronger EN alignment but poor XL transfer
  • SoLU's superposition-reduction inductive bias may aid cross-lingual generalization
  • ReLU/GELU perform on par with each other

05

Positional Encodings

TinyCausalTransformer · intfloat/multilingual-e5-small (ref)

Positional encodings provide transformers with sequential order information. We compare five PE strategies in a controlled setting and benchmark against a pretrained multilingual reference model.

Variants

Sinusoidal ★ Learned RoPE ALiBi XPOS

Key Findings

  • Pretrained backbone (multilingual-e5-small) dominates all controlled variants
  • Sinusoidal achieves the best alignment among controlled models
  • Learned PE is competitive but less stable across languages
  • RoPE, ALiBi, XPOS underperform without pretraining in this regime

About

Dataset

ds003643 — 84 subjects (49 EN, 35 CN) listening to The Little Prince. fMRI parcellated into 200 cortical regions (Schaefer atlas), TR = 2.0 s.

Pipeline

Story text → hidden-state extraction → hemodynamic lag stack (τ=1..5) → ridge regression → Pearson R per brain parcel.

Adaptation Strategies

SI (frozen), IT (fine-tuned), LoRA r=8/16/32, QLoRA (4-bit), DoRA (weight-decomposed).
